Which Creepers and Hanging Plants Are Best for Balconies?

Imagine stepping on your balcony after a long day. A cup of tea, cold air brushing past, and green leaves slowly flow upwards. Balconies don’t have to be small, but the right plants can turn them into miniatures of oases. Creepers and hanging flowers are perfect to do it. They take up minimal floor space, they thrive, and they add vibrancy to walls and rails.

But which are the most suitable for balconies? Therefore, that is what the majority of plant lovers are curious about. Let’s explore it together in a simple yet engaging way.

Why do balconies deserve green companions?

Balconies are simply neglected. Individuals treat them as extra storage spaces or simply do nothing with them. But they are full of potential. Greenery in these spaces is not just visually appealing but also:

  • Cool down the area naturally.
  • Filters air and brings freshness.
  • Form privacy with nature screens.
  • Bring the outdoors indoors for city apartments.

Furthermore, creepers and hanging plants are ideal for small balconies where large pots take up too much space. Instead of covering the floor, they trail on walls, run over railings, and suspend like a green curtain.

Creepers that add charm to small spaces

There are creepers that grow rapidly. There are creepers that bloom magnificently. There are creepers that need little care. The selection will depend on the mood you want your balcony to create.

  1. Money Plant
    Nearly every home has one. It’s purported to attract money, but even if you don’t believe in that, it’s lovely. It may be trained down balcony balustrades or allowed to fall freely from hanging baskets. Its heart-shaped green foliage lightens up even gloomy corners.
  2. Morning Glory
    As its name indicates, it greets you with cheerful flowers each morning. Purple, blue, or pink-colored, it is a creeper that propagates on its own. Some sunlight and a bit of support, and your balcony railing is colored.
  3. Bougainvillea
    This is a showstopper. Gorgeous magenta, orange, or white colors drape the plant for months. It prefers sun, so if your balcony sees bright light, bougainvillea can bring it to life with little effort.
  4. Sweet Potato Vine
    This is not for farms alone. Its dramatic leaves with star and heart shapes provide texture to spaces. It trails well and pairs well with flowers.
  5. Jasmine Creeper
    If you love fragrance, jasmine is the choice. It blooms in the night, infusing the balcony with a calming aroma. Ideal for tea time in the evening or late nights of restful time.

Hanging plants that create a cosy balcony vibe

Hanging baskets are like magic pots. They occupy less space, they are fashionable, and they allow plants to pour down like green chandeliers.

  1. Spider Plant
    This eternally beautiful plant never dies. Its tall striped leaves dangle majestically, and it’s also one of the best air cleaners. Perfect for beginners, as it requires very little care.
  2. String of Pearls
    Rare and dramatic, this succulent grows spherical pearl-shaped leaves that cascade over baskets like a strand of pearls. It is a sun-worshiper, so position it near the balcony railing.
  3. Petunias
    For color enthusiasts, petunias give you vibrant flowers in whatever color you desire. They spill over in a cluster, setting the balcony in a party mood.
  4. Ferns
    Bushy and graceful, ferns thrive in semi-shade. A soft, bower-like look they give, while tempering other more showy flowers. Even a single hanging fern can refresh an ordinary corner.
  5. English Ivy
    This creeper adds elegance with its small patterned leaves. It spreads endlessly and captivates modern or country-style balconies alike.

Tips to care for creepers and hanging plants

Balcony plants are cherished but also need a little bit of knowledge about their needs.

  • Light: Some plants love sun, while others prefer shade. Find out the light pattern of your balcony.
  • Watering: Hanging baskets tend to dry out quickly. Check the moisture levels often but not excessively as to waterlog. 
  • Support: Creepers need a frame or railing to hold on to.
  • Pruning: Prune regularly in order to keep them neat and under control.
  • Soil: Light soil with compost for better drainage and nutrients.
