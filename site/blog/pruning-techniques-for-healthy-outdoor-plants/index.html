Why is pruning important for outdoor plants?

Pruning removes dead, infected, or damaged branches. Pruning stops the spread of infection and insects. Pruning also facilitates flowering and new growth. Additionally, pruning also enhances light and air in the plant. So, plants are healthier and lovelier. Plants become weak and overgrown if they are not pruned. Furthermore, pruning sets plants in place for a neat garden appearance. So, pruning techniques should be learned by each gardener.

Basic tools for pruning

A good set of tools simplifies pruning and makes it safe. The following are the standard pruning tools:

  • Small branch hand pruners
  • Medium branch loppers
  • Thick branch pruning saw
  • Hand gloves to safeguard hands
  • Disinfectant for cleaning the tools before use

Different pruning techniques

There are certain pruning techniques, all serving a different purpose. Having knowledge of them keeps plants healthy.

  1. Thinning

Thinning involves removing whole branches at the base. It opens up the plant for light and air. It lightens the weight on the branches and breaks.

  1. Heading Back

Cutting half a branch is heading back. It produces bushier stems and more flowers.However, don’t cut so much that it deems the plant stressed.

  1. Pinching

Pinching destroys soft new shoots manually. The procedure causes miniature plants to become bushy. It is applicable to herbs and juvenile shrubs.

  1. Renewal Pruning

Renewal pruning removes old stems at the ground level. It promotes fresh growth. The procedure applies to overly large shrubs.

  1. Cleaning

Cleaning eliminates dead, diseased, or broken branches. It is the simplest pruning but highly essential.

When to prune outdoor plants?

Timing is important in the success of pruning. Varying plants need to be pruned at varying times.

  • Spring flowers: Prune after blooming.
  • Summer flowers: Prune late winter or early spring.
  • Evergreens: Prune at any time except in late fall.
  • Fruit trees: Prune in winter months when they are dormant.

Moreover, do not prune in extremely hot or freezing weather. Pruning at the appropriate time enables the plants to heal and grow well.

How to prune for plant health?

Pruning properly requires careful cutting and attention.

  • Cut at a 45-degree angle above a juncture of a bud or branch.
  • Cut out rubbing or crossing branches.
  • Cut out dead or diseased wood first.
  • Never cut more than one-third of the plant in one process.
  • Step back often to evaluate the shape.

Therefore, take your time and cut deliberately. If you purchase plants from Indore’s largest nursery, they will guide you on pruning.

Common mistakes to avoid while pruning

Errors in pruning hurt plants. Do not make these mistakes:

  • Pruning too close to the stem, cutting it
  • Pruning with big stubs that invite pests
  • Pruning too much at once, making the plant weak
  • Pruning with dirty, rusty tools that spread disease
  • Pruning during the wrong season, slowing growth
  • Over-pruning leaves the plant weak too. Prune sparingly.
