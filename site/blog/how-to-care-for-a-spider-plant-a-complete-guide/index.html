How to Care for a Spider Plant: A Complete Guide

Introduction

Spider plants are the most popular houseplants people love so much. They can be easily maintained, thus ideal for people who are starting out. They have long, green, stripy leaves, which are lovely to put in any room. Spider plants can thrive in various light and weather conditions. 

However, they also purify the air by removing toxic chemicals. They are hence a healthy option for the home and workspace. You can pot them or have them in hanging baskets. Additionally, they also produce baby plants, which you can replant. In this article, you will get easy and straightforward steps on how to take care of your spider plant and make it strong and healthy.

What is a spider plant?

A spider plant (Chlorophytum comosum) is a very low-maintenance and durable houseplant. It’s native to southern Africa and has long green leaves that hang over like a fountain. Furthermore, it develops tiny white blooms that become baby plants. The baby plants hang from the mother plant and look just like tiny spiders. That’s where it was named as a spider plant.

Spider plants are perfect for beginners as they require very little care. They can grow in most of the house and in all types of weather. You can plant them up or hang them in baskets. They also clean the air at home. You can purchase healthy spider plants from a plant nursery in Indore or the biggest nursery in Indore with various plants.

Spider plant care tips

Light Requirements

Spider plants like bright, indirect light. They can avoid low light, but their growth will decrease. Excessive direct sunlight will burn their leaves, and they will turn brown. Thus, it is necessary to place your spider plant in a place where it gets medium sunlight.

  • Keep it close to a window but not directly in the sun.
  • If your home receives very little natural light, artificial light can also be used.

Watering Spider plants

Spider plants are slightly moist like their soil but are not very wet. Excess water leads to root rot, which is also the most common problem with spider plants.

  • When the top inch of soil is dry, water your spider plant.
  • Make sure the pots will cover the drains properly from the drains so that there is no pool in the water bottom.
  • During winter, the growth of the plant will decrease as water cuts.

Soil and Fertilization

Spider plants such as well flowing soil. A good mixture is peat, perlite and pine bark. You can buy suitable soil in any Indore plant nursery. Feed your spider plant monthly when it is producing (spring and summer).

  • An equal balanced, water -soluble fertilizer can be used.
  • Do not excessively, or the plant will become lazy.

Spider plants often do not require recurrence. Only when the roots completely fill the utensils.

Temperature and humidity

Average room temperature between 60 ° F and 75 ° F (16 ° C and 24 ° C) such as spider plants. They also prefer moderate humidity but can bear dry air.

  • Avoid cold drafts and heat sources directly on your spider plant.
  • If you live in a dry climate, try to place the plant in a more humid place, such as a bathroom or kitchen.

Common problems and how to solve them

Spider plants are low maintenance, but at times they experience some issues. Some of the most common issues and how to treat them are listed below:

  • Brown tips of leaves: Brown tips on leaves are mostly due to underwatering, overwatering, or too direct sunlight. When you observe it, ensure that you are supplying water in the right way to the plant. Ensure that you give time for the top inch of water to dry out again before the water. Moreover, relocate your spider plant to a location where it gets bright indirect light and never directly under the sun because it will burn the leaves.
  • Yellow leaves: Yellow leaves show that the plant might receive excessive water or burning heat. If your plant is receiving excessive water, allow the water to dry before watering. Do not put your spider plant in front of the cold air, and put it at 60°F to 75 ° F (16°C to 24°C).
  • Leggy growth: If your spider plant has been grown and dispersed outside, it needs more light. Move it to a light place, or if it does not receive sufficient natural light, consider growing it under the developed light.
  • Pests: Spider plants may occasionally develop pests like aphids or mealybugs. If you observe that the plant is infested, spray it using a diluted mixture of pesticide soap or dish soap and water to kill it. Ensure that you gently wash the leaves so that you do not damage them.

Why should you have a spider plant at home?

Spider plants are not only aesthetically pleasing to look at—sophisticated creatures; they are also very convenient. Perhaps one of their greatest qualities is that they are air purifiers. They will eliminate poisonous chemicals such as carbon monoxide, formaldehyde, and other indoor pollutants. This purifies your home and makes it healthier.

Another excellent feature of spider plants is that they are cat- and dog-friendly. Very few houseplants can be handled by cats and dogs without poisoning them, but spider plants aren’t harmful to them. 


However, should you want a low-maintenance yet lovely plant, the spider plant is the best choice for you. It gives any room a touch of elegance with less effort.

Therefore,To complement this, spider plants are also extremely simple to propagate more of. They produce baby plants, or plantlets, that dangle on long stems. These can be cut off and planted to create more plants. With all these combined, spider plants are a simple and appropriate addition to any home.
