Top 5 DIY Composting Ideas for Natural Fertilizer

2025-04-18 · Indore Nursery Team

Composting is a great method of waste reduction and making natural fertilizer. It is organic waste recycling into fertile nutrients for the soil of your plants. If you want to have a green method of nourishing your garden, DIY composting is your solution. Let us discuss the top 5 DIY composting projects that can turn kitchen trash and yard clippings into successful plant food.

Composting Kitchen Scraps

Coffee grounds, vegetable peels, and fruit skins are great materials to compost. Begin by constructing a compost bin in the kitchen or the backyard outdoors. Combine the peels with dry leaves or cardboard, which are brown materials to be employed in balancing the compost. The peels will decay over time and turn into rich nutrient compost that your plants will enjoy.

To have your compost bin functioning properly, turn it from time to time. Turning accelerates the decomposition process and prevents bad odors. Try to refrain from dairy or meat since they will invite pests.

Composting with Yard Waste

Leaves, grass clippings, and twigs are great to use to compost yard clippings. Gather them over the year and pile them in a corner of your yard to form a compost heap. Additionally, add some kitchen trash to introduce variety into your compost. Grass clipping provides nitrogen, and dry leaves provide carbon, thereby achieving a good balance in the compost.

To enhance the process of composting, chop big twigs or branches into small pieces. Moreover, this enhances the surface area and accelerates decomposition. If you lack garden wastes, go to a plant nursery in Indore and collect organic wastes and put them in your compost bin.

Vermicomposting with Worms

Vermicomposting refers to the decomposition of organic trash into nutrients using worms. Red worms are the most popular worms utilized for vermicomposting. The red worms decompose food trash and recycle it into dense worm casts that are rich in nutrients. A vermicomposting system is easy to make in limited space a favorite in urban areas.

To start vermicomposting, create a bin with holes for ventilation. Add a bedding layer of newspaper, cardboard, or leaves. Therefore, introduce the worms and start feeding them food scraps like fruit peels, coffee grounds, and vegetable trimmings. The worms will do the hard work of breaking down the waste. However, you’ll have a supply of worm castings to use as natural fertilizer for your plants.

Composting Using a Compost Tumbler

If you prefer to have a structured way of composting, then a compost tumbler would suit you. A compost tumbler makes it easy for you to turn and aerate the compost, speeding up the decomposition process. You can purchase a compost tumbler or create one using wood or plastic barrel materials.

To compost using the tumbler, simply fill it full of your kitchen waste, grass clippings, and some soil. Seal the tumbler and turn it every few days to aerate the compost. However, this process makes compost faster than other processes, usually in a few months. Composting in a tumbler also makes your compost clean and pest-free.

For individuals who do not have access to a garden or outdoors, indoor composting is ideal. Indoor composting occurs indoors in a smaller space, such as an apartment or on a balcony, through indoor compost containers or bokashi systems. Bokashi compost is made by fermenting the food wastes and will decompose into compost.

Moreover, you can compost within an indoor outdoor plants nursery in Indore. The nurseries usually have some space where you can gather organic matter to be composted. The benefit is that nurseries usually have tremendous plant experience and can advise you to know what best to use in composting.

Benefits of DIY Composting

DIY composting is of great assistance, aside from the fact that it creates fertilizer. It first saves the waste that goes into landfills. Composting also enhances the soil structure and holds water, thus decreasing the watering need. Composting also slows soil erosion and enhances the garden ecosystem.

If you want to grow in compost soil, you can grow air-purifying plants for summer in Indore. Furthermore, not only do these plants clean the air, but they also accept natural fertilizer, which you produce when you compost. Composting produces a healthy, sustainable world for human beings and plants alike.
