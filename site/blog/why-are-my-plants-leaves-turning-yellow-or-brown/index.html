Why are my plant’s leaves turning yellow or brown?

2025-05-02

Introduction

It’s upsetting to see your plant leaves change color to brown or yellow. These color changes can be due to so many different things. Plants are delicate and will react to their environment. As a plant owner, it’s comforting to know why it occurs. You can most frequently correct the problem with proper care and make your plants flourish.

This blog shall walk you through the most common reasons for yellow or brown leaves and offer solutions. It shall assist you in figuring out the cause of the problem and how you can prevent issues in the future and get the best plants for home decor in Indore.

Common causes of yellow or brown leaves

There are many reasons that the leaves of the plant are yellow. Let’s go on to the most frequent reasons:

Overwatering

One of the most frequent causes of yellow or brown leaves is overwatering. When the plants are overwatered, the roots do not get enough oxygen. The roots rot as a result. Symptoms of overwatering include yellow leaves and waterproof soil.

  • The roots suffocate because they lack oxygen.
  • The leaves turn yellow or brown.
  • The soil remains very wet.

However, to avoid this, make sure that your pot has drainage holes. Water plants only when the top inches of soil feel dry.

Underwatering

Additionally, underwatering also causes yellow or brown leaves. Plants need water to carry out their normal functions. Without enough moisture, they cannot function properly, leading to dry and browning leaves.

  • Hard and cracked soil is caused by the drying out of the soil.
  • Yellow leaves become yellow before they turn brown.
  • Wilting or drooping of the plant.

Be sure to water your plants on a regular basis. Inspect the ground for moisture and adjust your watering schedule accordingly.

Poor Soil Drainage

Even when watering properly, waterlogged soil is detrimental to your plants. When the soil is not draining, water rests next to the roots. It will waterlog your plants and rot roots or turn yellow or brown leaves pale. 

  • Waterlogged roots are devoid of oxygen.
  • Roots start to rot.
  • Leaves on plants turn yellow, then brown.

Moreover, to rectify, repot your plant in draining soil. Mix perlite or sand to drain better.

Nutrient Deficiencies

Nutrient deficiency can lead to brown or yellow leaves. Plants need essential nutrients like nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium so that they become robust. Without these nutrients, their leaves turn colored.

  • Yellowing of leaves can be due to a lack of nitrogen.
  • Edge browning could be indicative of a potassium deficiency.
  • Yellowing of veins is due to a lack of iron.

To fix this, apply a balanced fertilizer. Therefore, use it when the plant requires it. Organic fertilizers are always the best at promoting good development in plants.

Identifying the specific problem

Deciding on the specific cause of yellow or brown leaves can not always be easy. But your close observation or taking help from the best plant nursery in Indore for your plant will help you to make this decision. This is how you do it.

  • Feel the soil: Slowly touch the soil with your fingers. Is it wet or dry? If your plant is in a waterfall, the soil will become wet. If it is dry, the soil will be dry.
  • Check the leaves: Does the leaf margin turn brown? This is an indication that the plant is overwatered. If the entire leaf is yellow, it is either an indication that the plant is overwatered or lacks nutrients.
  • Look for insects: Sometimes, a plant will be covered with insects such as aphids or spider mites. These are prone to removing leaves.
  • Check the environment: Is your plant being provided enough light? Either too much or too little light can obstruct its goodness.

Once you identify the cause, you can take action to resolve the issue.

How to fix yellow or brown leaves?

Having realized the problem, the following are the solutions:

If Overwatering is the Problem:

  • Let the soil dry out completely.
  • Repot the plant in fresh, draining soil.
  • Cut out rotten roots and do not water for a few days.

If Underwatering is the Problem:

  • Water the plant well.
  • Be careful to ensure that the soil does not dry excessively.
  • Create a routine watering schedule.

If Poor Soil Drainage is the Problem

  • Repot your plant in a pot that drains well with holes for drainage.
  • Use well-draining soil to avoid root rot.
  • Avoid waterlogging your plant.

If Nutrient Deficiencies are the Problem:

  • Feed with the proper nutrients, according to the deficiency.
  • Select organic fertilizers to maintain your plant’s health.

Following the recommended guidelines for feeding your plant.

Preventing future problems

It is always better to avoid creating problems rather than solving them. Here are some tips to keep your plants healthy and prevent yellowing or browning of leaves:

  • Water properly: Ensure that your plant receives the proper amount of water. Either a lack of water or too much water will harm your plant.
  • Provide the appropriate light: Give the proper amount of light to your plant. Direct sunlight is necessary for some and indirect light for others.
  • Repot if needed: Plants while growing sometimes need a larger container so that they grow properly. Repot plants in a larger container, providing enough space for them.
  • Supply good-quality soil: Healthy soil is the most vital for plants. Select soil that gives good drainage and nourishment.
